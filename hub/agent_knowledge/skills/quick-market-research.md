# Quick market/competitive research (before building)

A practical checklist for the hour of research worth doing before
committing to build an idea — not a full market study, just enough to not
build something that already exists or that nobody's shown interest in.

- **Search for the idea as a user would phrase the problem, not as you'd
  phrase the solution** — "how do I track X" surfaces existing tools and
  forum complaints; "X tracker app" mostly surfaces existing products'
  marketing pages. Do both; they answer different questions.
- **If 3+ direct competitors exist and are still operating, the question
  isn't "should I build this" — it's "what's the wedge"**: find the
  specific complaint their users post in reviews/forums (too expensive, missing
  a specific feature, bad for a specific use case) — that complaint is your
  actual differentiation, not a vaguer version of their pitch.
- **If ZERO existing tools show up, treat that as a flag to investigate,
  not a green light** — check whether it's because the problem is smaller
  than it seems, whether it's a solved problem people just don't search
  for by that name, or whether it's genuinely novel (rare, but does
  happen) — five minutes checking which one it is saves a wasted build.
- **Cheapest real validation signal, in order of effort**: does a search
  for the problem show real people complaining about it (free, 10 min) →
  will 5 people you can actually reach say yes to "would you use this
  tomorrow if it existed" specifically, not "sounds cool" (an hour) → a
  landing page collecting real signups before the build exists (a day) —
  each step is cheap enough to do before writing the first line of code.
- **Pricing research**: find 2-3 comparable tools' actual pricing pages,
  not just their existence — what they charge tells you the market's
  actual willingness to pay far better than reasoning from your own costs.
- **Write down what you found, even briefly** — "3 competitors, cheapest
  charges $12/mo, common complaint is X" — this becomes the spec's
  differentiation section instead of getting re-derived (or forgotten)
  later.
